Prefix Algebras

Note

This is an on-demand module. Load it with using OrbitAl.palg.

A PAlg is an algebra $A$, free of rank $d$ over a commutative ring $\Lambda$, given by generators $p_1, \dots, p_r$ and a basis of positive words in them,

\[b_1 = 1, \quad \dots, \quad b_d, \qquad b_x = b_{\mathrm{parent}(x)} T_{\mathrm{letter}(x)},\]

closed under prefixes. What is stored is the sparse multiplication by one generator on each side, $b_x T_j$ and $T_j b_x$; every other product is built from those. The algebra need not commute, so the two tables are different data.

Each generator has a minimal polynomial, params[j] = [c_0, …, c_{k-1}] with the constant term first,

\[T_j^k = c_{k-1} T_j^{k-1} + \dots + c_1 T_j + c_0,\]

and $c_0$ is a unit, which is what makes $T_j^{-1}$ an element of $A$. Generators that are to share a minimal polynomial share a vector.

The coefficient type carries the ground ring — Zn{p} for a run over $\mathbb{F}_p$, a polynomial type for a symbolic one — and nothing in the module branches on which. Zero and one are taken from a parameter instance, not from zero(T), so a coefficient type that defines them only on instances still works.

Against HeckeAlg

Both are algebras with a basis indexed by words, and they serve opposite sizes. HeckeAlg stores no elements and computes products from reduced words and the root action, so $W$ can be as large as $E_8$; it is the Iwahori-Hecke algebra of a Coxeter group and the quadratic relation is the same for every generator. A PAlg stores both multiplication tables, so its basis has to be held in memory — but the generators may have minimal polynomials of different degrees, the group behind it need not be a Coxeter group, and a product is a table lookup rather than a walk.

The product

mul(A, x, y) expands along the prefixes of the words in the support of y, or along the suffixes of the words in the support of x, whichever is cheaper. The memo then costs one generator multiplication per word reached rather than one per pair of basis elements, which is what makes a rank in the tens of thousands workable.

Products accumulate on a dense buffer and become an element once, at the end, so the cost is the support and not the rank. The buffers are pooled and indexed by depth, because a product may be computed while a caller holds one — mul itself does that along its memo. A single shared buffer would be silently corrupted and the answer would look plausible. Nothing here is threaded and the pool does not pretend to be thread-safe.

The cyclic case

cyclicAlg(params) is the one-generator case: the basis is $1, u, \dots, u^{d-1}$, the algebra is commutative, the two multiplication tables coincide, and the product is a convolution followed by a fold through the minimal polynomial. ugen, uinv, upow and squareParams belong to it alone and assert iscyclic, so a general algebra handed to a caller that wants $u^k$ is an error rather than a wrong answer.

Units

A basis element is a product of generators, hence a unit, and so is a product of one with the inverse of another. findUnitInverse recognises $c b$, $c b^{-1} b'$ and $c b b'^{-1}$, and verifies every candidate by multiplying it out. It is the test a search for an invertible pivot wants where the norm through multMatrix is correct and too expensive — the determinant of a $d \times d$ matrix, at the ranks this module is for.

Which coefficients count as units of $\Lambda$ depends on the ground ring, so the test is an argument: !iszero over a field, isunit over Zn{n}, something narrower where only part of $\Lambda$ is inverted.

julia> using OrbitAl.palg

julia> A = cyclicAlg([3//1, 5//1])        # u^2 = 5u + 3
Z2

julia> mul(A, ugen(A), ugen(A))
(3)b1 + (5)b2

julia> mul(A, uinv(A), ugen(A)) == one(A)
true

julia> tau(A, one(A))
1//1

julia> squareParams([3//1, 5//1])         # the minimal polynomial of u^2
2-element Vector{Rational{Int64}}:
 -9
 31

Algebras and Elements

OrbitAl.palg.PAlg — Type
PAlg{T}

An algebra on a prefix-closed basis of positive words, with the right and left multiplications by each generator stored.

params[j] = [c_0, .., c_(k-1)], constant term first, is the minimal polynomial of the generator p_j: T^k = c_(k-1) T^(k-1) + .. + c_1 T + c_0. Generators that share a minimal polynomial share a vector.

PElt{T}

An element of a PAlg, sum val[k] b_(idx[k]) with idx strictly increasing and val free of zeros. That form is canonical, so equality is structural.

source

PAlg(name, params, words, right, left)

The algebra on words, which must be closed under prefixes and ordered so that a word comes after its prefix.

cyclicAlg(params)

The algebra of one generator u: the basis is 1, u, .., u^(d-1) and the algebra is commutative, so the two multiplication tables coincide. params = [c_0, .., c_(d-1)], constant term first, and c_0 must be a unit since u^-1 is read off it.

OrbitAl.palg.squareParams — Function
squareParams(params)

The minimal polynomial of u^2 from that of u, params constant term first, same degree d.

Splitting m into even and odd parts, m(x) = E(x^2) + x O(x^2),

prod_i (y - xi_i^2) = (-1)^d m(sqrt y) m(-sqrt y)
                    = (-1)^d (E(y)^2 - y O(y)^2)

which is the characteristic polynomial of multiplication by u^2 and annihilates u^2 by Cayley-Hamilton.

The constant term is (-1)^(d+1) c_0^2, a unit exactly when c_0 is.

It is the minimal polynomial unless two roots of m differ by a sign; otherwise it is a proper multiple of it.
