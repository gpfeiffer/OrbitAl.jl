Streets and Descent Algebras

Note

This is an on-demand module. Load it with using OrbitAl.streets.

The alleys of a finite Coxeter group $W$ with simple reflections $S$ are the paths in the Hasse diagram of the power set of $S$; the streets are the orbits of a conjugation action of the free monoid $S^*$ on the alleys. The streets span the street algebra $\Xi$, a path algebra, which maps onto Solomon's descent algebra $\Sigma(W)$ by the anti-homomorphism $\Delta$. See G. Pfeiffer, A quiver presentation for Solomon's descent algebra, Adv. Math. 220 (2009) 1428–1465.

Everything is combinatorial: from $W$ only the opposition involutions $s \mapsto w_J s w_J$ of the parabolic subsets $J$ are used. Subsets are bit masks, and the alleys are numbered without a table, so that the rank can be as large as memory allows (type $D_9$, with $2\,681\,216$ alleys, takes about ten seconds).

OrbitAl.streets.StreetSystem — Type
StreetSystem(W)

The alleys, streets and shapes of the finite Coxeter group W (a CoxeterGp). The streets are the orbits of the conjugation action of the free monoid on the alleys [Pf09, Section 6]; the shapes are the streets of length 0.

Examples

julia> using OrbitAl, OrbitAl.coxeter, OrbitAl.streets

julia> X = StreetSystem(CoxeterGp(cartanMat("A", 3)));

julia> (nrAlleys(X), nrStreets(X), nrShapes(X))
(38, 27, 5)

OrbitAl.streets.xiQuiver — Function
xiQuiver(X)

The Ext-quiver of the street algebra $\Xi$ of X: a named tuple with the number of arrows arrows[(lam, mu)] from shape lam to shape mu (in the direction of the streets, from source to target), the Cartan invariants cartan[(lam, mu)], and ispath, which says whether $\Xi$ is the path algebra of this quiver, i.e., whether the Cartan invariants count its paths. (It always is: $\Xi$ is hereditary.)

Examples

julia> using OrbitAl, OrbitAl.coxeter, OrbitAl.streets

julia> q = xiQuiver(StreetSystem(CoxeterGp(cartanMat("A", 3))));

julia> (sum(values(q.arrows)), q.ispath)
(8, true)

OrbitAl.streets.descentQuiver — Function
descentQuiver(X)

The quiver of the descent algebra $\Sigma(W)$ of X, computed with [Pf09, Algorithm 10.1]: a named tuple with the number of arrows arrows[(lam, mu)] for each pair of shapes (in the direction of the streets, from source lam to target mu; [Pf09] draws them from mu to lam), and dimrad, the dimension of the radical, which is $2^n - |\Lambda|$.

Examples

julia> using OrbitAl, OrbitAl.coxeter, OrbitAl.streets

julia> q = descentQuiver(StreetSystem(CoxeterGp(cartanMat("A", 3))));

julia> (sum(values(q.arrows)), q.dimrad)
(2, 3)

OrbitAl.streets.descentPresentation — Function
descentPresentation(X; arrows = :orthogonal)

A presentation of the descent algebra $\Sigma(W)$ of X as a quiver with relations, by [Pf09, Algorithm 10.1].

The arrows from lam to mu are elements of $\Xi$ whose images span a complement of $\Delta(\mathrm{rad}^2 \Xi)$ in $\Delta(\mathrm{rad}\, \Xi)$ in that block. With arrows = :orthogonal (the default), this is the orthogonal complement for the inner product in which the basis $e_J$ of $\Sigma(W)$ [Pf09, Proposition 9.2] is orthonormal (restricted to $\Sigma(W) e_L$ for the representative $L$ of lam), with the basis in reduced echelon form in the coordinates $e_J$. This choice does not depend on a choice of streets. With arrows = :streets, each arrow is the first street of its block that is independent modulo $\Delta(\mathrm{rad}^2 \Xi)$.

Returns a named tuple with

  • arrows: a list of triples (comb, source, target), one for each arrow of the quiver, in the direction of the streets (from source to target), where comb is the arrow as a linear combination of streets (a Dict, with coefficients mod $2^{31} - 1$);
  • paths: for each pair (lam, mu) of shapes, the list of all paths from lam to mu, as lists of arrow numbers;
  • relations: for each pair (lam, mu), a basis of the relations among the paths from lam to mu that are not consequences of relations between shorter paths, in reduced echelon form, as Dicts from path numbers to rational coefficients (nothing if rational reconstruction fails);
  • allrelations: the dimensions of the full spaces of relations.

Paths compose like streets: a path [e1, e2] is the product $\alpha_1 \circ \alpha_2$ in $\Xi$, which $\Delta$ maps to $f_{\alpha_2} f_{\alpha_1}$, as $\Delta$ is an anti-homomorphism.

Examples

julia> using OrbitAl, OrbitAl.coxeter, OrbitAl.streets

julia> P = descentPresentation(StreetSystem(CoxeterGp(cartanMat("A", 5))));

julia> (length(P.arrows), sum(length, values(P.relations)))
(9, 1)
